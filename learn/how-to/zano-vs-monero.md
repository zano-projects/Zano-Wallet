**Short answer:** Both are private; Zano adds staking, aliases and confidential assets, while Monero focuses on a single private coin.

## You'll see this if

- You hold XMR and are curious about Zano
- You want staking on a privacy coin

## Why it happens

Different design goals lead to different features.

## How to fix it

1. List the features you need.
2. Compare staking, aliases and assets.
3. Try Zano Wallet with a small amount.
4. Keep backups for both.
5. Decide by your use case.

Related reading: [How Zano Keeps Transactions Private](./zano-privacy-how-it-works.md) and the [Zano vs Monero comparison](https://zanowallet.io/vs/monero/).

## If that didn't work

See the full comparison on the website. You can also read [Zano Wallet vs Cake Wallet](./zano-vs-cake-wallet.md).

## Prevent it next time

Never move seeds between apps; create a new wallet and transfer on-chain. More on the website: [Monero GUI comparison](https://zanowallet.io/vs/monero-gui/).

## FAQ

**Can I use one wallet for both?**  
No, each coin uses its own wallet.

**Is Zano faster?**  
Block times differ; see the comparison.
