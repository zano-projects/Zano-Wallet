**Short answer:** Keep the seed phrase on paper offline and a copy of the .wallet file on an external drive.

## You'll see this if

- You just created a wallet
- You hold more than you can afford to lose

## Why it happens

Hardware fails and laptops get stolen; a backup turns a disaster into a short restore.

## How to fix it

1. Write the seed on paper, clearly and in order.
2. Store it away from the computer.
3. Copy the .wallet file to a USB drive.
4. Label it and keep it separate from the seed.
5. Test a restore on a spare install once.

Related reading: [Create a New Zano Wallet Step by Step](./create-new-zano-wallet.md) and the [security checklist](https://github.com/zano-projects/Zano-Wallet/tree/main/learn).

## If that didn't work

If you never recorded the seed, create a new wallet, back it up properly and send your coins to it. You can also read [Restore a Zano Wallet From Your Seed Phrase](./restore-zano-wallet-from-seed.md).

## Prevent it next time

Review backups every time you update or change computers. More guides: [self-custody explained](https://github.com/zano-projects/Zano-Wallet/tree/main/learn).

> [!WARNING]
> Nobody from Zano Wallet will ever ask for your seed phrase. Never type it into a website or chat.

## FAQ

**Should I store the seed in a password manager?**  
Paper offline is safer for large amounts.

**Do I back up after every transaction?**  
No; the seed covers all future transactions.
